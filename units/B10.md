# B10 · Transporting the lattice inequality to a gap

AI-written, AI cross-reviewed; no human sign-off; not a claim that RH is proved

The lattice inequality can be read as an upper bound on an arithmetic gap at each selected translate. This requires bijections matching the top and place indices, matching coefficients, and explicit bounds on counting and slope errors. It supplies the gap that the character average consumes.

## Statement

Take finite units $U_0$, nonempty finite word sets $W(u)$, finite top indices, and lattice data with $m$ columns and $f$ places. Take a finite abelian group $H$, a map $\operatorname{tr}:U_0\to H$, and arithmetic data as in [B09](B09.md). Assume top and place bijections identify the coefficients $a_t$, column weights, Euler constant and $\Re\ell(\chi_C)$, and express the lattice $\mathrm{ASK}(u)$ as the sum of its arithmetic terms at $\operatorname{tr}u$. Assume the eleven lattice hypotheses of [P021](P021.md) and the following bounds on the data. With $x\ge1$, $0<\varepsilon\le1$, $b\ge1$, $xb\le a$, $n_T\ge1$, $m\le x^{1/2+\varepsilon/2}$, $xm\le k$, one has

$$
1\le k\le\min(a,x^{e_K}),\quad 0\le L,L_a\le2x^\varepsilon,\quad 0\le kb'\le a,\quad 0\le X''\le a,\quad 0\le a_t\le2a .
$$

Also $e_K\ge0$, $\log|W(u)|\le(k-3)\log2$, $\bar\delta\le1/2$, $\bar\eta,\bar e\ge0$, and $(\bar\eta+4\sqrt{\bar e})x^{c_{HH}}b\le Kx^\varepsilon n_Ta$. The seven constants controlling division, the $E$ and $H$ errors, counting, slope, first-slope and loss bounds are in $[0,K]$; the exponents $e_{\rm Div},e_{\rm Loss},c_{HH}$ are nonnegative. Finally

$$
\begin{gathered} 0\le B_0\le 2nmbx^\varepsilon,\qquad 0\le N_c\le n_2 k b x^\varepsilon/(n^2x^{-\varepsilon}),\\ \max\{\log x,200(1+K)^2(1+c_{HH})(1+e_K)\}\le x^\varepsilon . \end{gathered}
$$

For each $u\in U_0$ the gap at $\operatorname{tr}u$ is at most $x^{(e_{\rm Div}+e_{\rm Loss}+3)\varepsilon}\mathrm{Br}$ with $a_E=a$.

## Assumptions

- Use finite unit and nonempty word-fibre data, the finite group, translation and the two specified bijections matching coefficients, weights, character constants and ASK.
- Assume the eleven lattice conditions for these data and the ErrWindows, $B_0,N_c$ and large-scale inequalities in the statement.
