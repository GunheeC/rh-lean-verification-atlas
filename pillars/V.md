## The assertion and its Lean entry point

Fix a primitive complex Dirichlet character $\psi$ of positive modulus, $0<\varepsilon<1/52$ and **any** function $e:\mathbb R\to\mathbb N$ with $x^{2+8\varepsilon}\le 2^{e(x)}\le 2x^{2+8\varepsilon}$ for every $x\ge1$. There exist a real $J\ge1$ and $X_0\in\mathbb R$ such that for every real $X\ge X_0$ and every window pair $(q,d)$ at $X^{1/J}$ there exists $Y\in\mathbb R$ with $X^{1/J}\le Y$ and $Y^2\le X$. For **every** $x\in[Y,Y^2]$, let $t=\lfloor x^{401}\rfloor_+$. For **every** number field $E$ of the indicated cyclotomic type, every algebraic closure $\Omega/E$, and every system $D=(D_\ell)_\ell$ of allowed local data, all lengths $h_\ell(k)$ are finite at all primes $\ell$ and all integers $1\le k<t$. For every finite set $S$ of primes,
$$
 \sum_{\ell\in S}\log(\ell)\sum_{1\le k<t}h_\ell(k)
 \le \sqrt{\exp(x^\varepsilon)}.
$$
Here $h_\ell(k)$ is the $\mathbb Z_\ell$-length of the kernel of a localization map at the prime $\ell$ and twist $k$. The next section specifies the map, the prime-pair test and the cyclotomic field test. The field type uses the actual $e(x),q,d$. `PChar` (`PrimePairSizeCondition.lean:7–11`) `PageN` (`ExistsPageN.lean:39–39`) `VVle` (`ExistsPageN.lean:12–17`) `VAt` (`ExistsPageN.lean:41–42`) `VWinAt` (`ExistsPageN.lean:44–47`) `VWin` (`ExistsPageN.lean:49–49`)

## What is tested after the interval has been chosen?

**Prime pair.** Put $y=X^{1/J}$ and $H=y^{\varepsilon/5}$. The primes satisfy $q\equiv3\pmod8$, $d\equiv7\pmod {16}$,
$$
 H/2<\log q\le H,\qquad H(\log y)^2/2<\log d\le H(\log y)^2 .
$$
One requires $q\mid d+1$, and $p\mid d+1$ for every odd prime $p\le H$. This is an implication for each supplied pair. A proof that pairs exist at the required scales is a separate input when another theorem wants to choose a pair. In particular the scale in the size restrictions is determined before $Y$ is chosen. `WinPair` (`ExistsPageN.lean:27–37`) `VWinAt` (`ExistsPageN.lean:44–47`)

**Cyclotomic field type.** For the given $e=e(x)$, the field predicate asks for a positive natural number $N\in\mathbb N$, a rational algebra embedding $j:E\hookrightarrow\mathbb Q(\zeta_N)$ and a primitive $N$th root $\zeta$ there, with $2^{e+2},N_\psi,q,d$ all dividing $N$. For every rational automorphism $\tau$ of this cyclotomic field and every $a\in\mathbb N$ with $\tau\zeta=\zeta^a$, the assertion that $\tau$ fixes $j(E)$ pointwise is **equivalent** to the conjunction
$$
 a\equiv \pm1\pmod {2^{e+2}},\quad \psi(a)=1,\quad
 \left(\frac a q\right)=\left(\frac a d\right)=1.
$$
The characters in the last display are the two Jacobi symbols. Testing one convenient cyclotomic model verifies only that model unless a comparison handles every field having the specified fixing group. The universal field binder of the window follows the choice of $x$. `IsChiField` (`ExistsPageN.lean:19–25`) `val` (`PrimePairSizeCondition.lean:17–17`) `VAt` (`ExistsPageN.lean:41–42`)

**Integral lengths.** At prime $\ell$, choose the finite set of places above $\ell$, an extension of the valuation to $\Omega$ at every such place, and the specified cyclotomic action on the Galois quotient. These are fields of $D_\ell$. The twist on the divisible coefficient module is by the inverse $k$th power of the action. The source takes the $\mathbb Z_\ell$-length of the localization kernel in degree one and allows the value $\top$. Finiteness comes before converting each length to a natural number in the sum. A bound proved after replacing infinite lengths by their natural-number conversion alone would miss the first conjunct in the actual record. The finite set may contain $2$. `LData` (`Statement.lean:54–63`) `qzTw` (`Statement.lean:67–69`) `Sha` (`Statement.lean:75–76`) `shaLen` (`Statement.lean:78–78`) `VVle` (`ExistsPageN.lean:12–17`)

Here $S_\ell$ consists exactly of the finite places above $\ell$. The group `GS` quotients the global Galois group by the closed normal subgroup generated by inertia away from $S_\ell$. For each chosen place over $v\in S_\ell$, `locFam` maps its decomposition group into this quotient. The submodule `Sha1` intersects the kernels of the degree-one restriction maps over those $v$. Hence the localization in this length uses the places above $\ell$, which differ from the freely chosen finite set of rational primes in the final sum.
