**Exceptional primes of the same host, D014**

For every frame and every host satisfying $\mathrm{HostAx}$ on it, put $Q=\mathrm{QS}$, $N=Qqd$, $S=\{q\}\cup\{p:p\mid Q,\ p\text{ prime}\}$. Let $I_p,F_p$ denote the inertia subgroup and Frobenius element of its arithmetic datum. Then $d$ is prime, $2,q\in S$, $d\notin S$, and, for prime $p$,

$$

 \begin{array}{ll}
 p\notin S\cup\{d\}:& I_p=\{0\},\\
 p\in S:& I_p\subseteq\Phi,\quad F_p\in\Phi,\\
 p=d:& I_d=\{0,s_0\},\qquad s_0=((1,0),0).
 \end{array}

$$

Here $\Phi$ consists of the elements with first binary coordinate zero.

**Proof.**

The host has $q,d$ prime, $q\equiv3\pmod8$, $d\equiv7\pmod{16}$, $(\frac{-d}{q})=1$, $2\mid Q$, and $(Q,qd)=1$. The two residues modulo eight are distinct, so $q\ne d$. Since $Qq\ne0$, $S$ is exactly the set of prime divisors of $Qq$; this proves the four assertions on $d,2,q,S$. For prime $p$, write $N^{(p)}=N/p^{v_p(N)}$. The inertia subgroup is the image under $r=\operatorname{toE}\circ\operatorname{art}$ (in additive coordinates) of the units $u$ modulo $N$ with $u\equiv1\pmod{N^{(p)}}$. The Frobenius unit has residues $p$ modulo $N^{(p)}$ and $1$ modulo $p^{v_p(N)}$; these specify one unit by the Chinese remainder theorem. Its image is $F_p$. By the host character law, the first binary coordinate of $r(u)$ is zero precisely when the Jacobi symbol $(\frac{u}{d})$ is $+1$: this coordinate encodes the second multiplicative sign of $\operatorname{art}(u)$. `HostHyp`, `FrobeniusOrbits/Statement.lean:13–14`; `HostRaw`, `HostDer.art_ch`, `ConjEMemHostFIff.lean:12–52,99`; `recH`, `arith`, `PrimitiveCosets/RecH.lean:10–23`; `mem_localUnits_iff`, `frobUnit_modEq`, `mem_inertia_iff`, `GaloisMapComplexConjugation.lean:33–40,86–89,123–128`.

If $p\notin S\cup\{d\}$, then $p\nmid N$, hence $N^{(p)}=N$. Only the identity unit contributes to inertia, giving $I_p=\{0\}$. If $p\in S$, then $d\mid N^{(p)}$. Every inertia unit is $1$ modulo $d$ and hence has positive symbol. The symbol of the Frobenius unit is $(\frac p d)$. For $p=2$ this is $+1$ since $d\equiv7\pmod8$. For odd $p\mid Qq$, quadratic reciprocity and $d\equiv3\pmod4$ give $(\frac p d)=(\frac{-d}p)$: the reciprocity sign $(-1)^{(p-1)/2}$ equals $(\frac{-1}p)$. The right side is $+1$ by the host's splitting hypothesis for odd $p\mid Q$, and by its defining condition for $p=q$. This proves both inclusions in $\Phi$. `jacobi_neg_recip`, `TotientGaloisClasses/HostFacts.lean:15–24`; `jac_two_d`, `GaloisMapComplexConjugation.lean:290–295`; `inPhi_of_jac`, `jac_of_dvd`, `d_dvd_ordCompl`, `PrimitiveCosets/hostFactsS.lean:7–38`.

Finally $N^{(d)}=Qq$, since $d\nmid Qq$. If $u\equiv1\pmod{Qq}$, the host law $\operatorname{art}_d$ restricts its Artin image to $1$ or the frame involution $\mathrm{iod}$, which map to $0$ and $s_0$. The identity unit realizes $0$. The coprime moduli $Qq,d$ admit a unit congruent to $1$ modulo $Qq$ and $-1$ modulo $d$. This is an inertia unit whose symbol is $(\frac{-1}{d})=-1$; its Artin image cannot be the identity, so it realizes $s_0$. This proves equality at $d$ and exhausts the fields of $\mathrm{HostFactsS}$. `HostRaw.art_d`, `ConjEMemHostFIff.lean:45–46`; `toE_bij`, `PrimitiveCosets/ToEBij.lean:37–39,67–71`; `exists_unit_crt`, `GaloisMapComplexConjugation.lean:47–61`; `hostFactsS_pf`, `PrimitiveCosets/hostFactsS.lean:44–125`.
