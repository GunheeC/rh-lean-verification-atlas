## From cuspidals to all irreducible classes

At a cuspidal class $y$, `GlobalTheory.RecT.GoodCusp` means rank one, or a continuous field isomorphism from some $\mathbb Q_g$ to the base together with a smooth character twist of a class having a nonzero $K_1$-fixed vector. A class satisfies `GlobalTheory.RecT.Good` when it admits a cuspidal support every member of which satisfies this test (`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/NonGaloisDescent/RecTExists.lean:19–23`). This yields a weaker assignment than the full local Langlands correspondence. Outside good cuspidals it permits an auxiliary irreducible parameter of the correct rank with a norm twist. The multisegment suppliers `UpperTheory.Zero.LLC.Pr.exh` and `UpperTheory.Zero.LLC.Pr.multi` are closed in-repository constants (`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/LocalLanglands/Multi.lean:11–12`). The construction calls the in-repository constants `goodMap`, `junkEx`, `expo` and `parUniq` (`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/LocalLanglands/LocalCentralUnits/CuspEx.lean:127–127,161–176`).

First choose an Artin map $a$. The construction **artEx** starts with local Artin existence. It also proves uniqueness by mapping both candidates through a common global Artin law, then using injectivity of the local idele comparison. The proof of B04 retains $a$ and its Artin property. It discards this additional uniqueness witness. Keeping the same $a$ throughout determines which character is used when a representation or a parameter is twisted. See `ArtEx.lean:7-15` and `LocalCentralUnits/RecTExists.lean:20-26`.

Next, **cuspEx** supplies an assignment $r$ with the cuspidal laws for this $a$. Its construction makes the scope of the theorem important. On the good cuspidal classes it uses **goodMap**. On the complement it uses a chosen auxiliary parameter of the right rank with a real norm twist. For a cuspidal class outside the good class the rank is at least two, since rank one is good. The argument checks irreducibility and dimension separately on the two branches. For the inflated types in the final law the depth and local-field conditions put the class back on the good branch. Thus the strong character and type compatibility has a definite place to enter the proof. See `CuspEx.lean:168-193`, `CuspEx.lean:258-266`.

Extend $r$ by multisegments. Exhaustion supplies a multisegment for each irreducible class $x$. To each segment attach its prescribed special Weil–Deligne summand, built from the parameter of the first cuspidal class with the segment shift. Sum over the multisegment. If another list presents $x$ as the same Langlands quotient, the classification gives equality of the two multisets. The direct sum is invariant under reordering, so both lists give the same parameter $r_c(x)$. For cuspidal $x$ one may use the one-term list consisting of the singleton segment of $x$. Its shift is $(1-1)/2=0$, the special block has length one, and summing the resulting parameter with the zero parameter returns $r(x)$. These are precisely the existence and restriction conclusions of **exists_isLExt**, `LanglandsExtensionUniqueOnCuspidal.lean:34-56`.

## Which compatibilities survive extension?

The returned record uses the equality $r_c=r$ on cuspidal classes in three distinct places. It gives the rank-one formula from the law for characters. Applied to every segment head, it puts the multisegment formula into the convention using $r_c$ itself. It also transports irreducibility for every cuspidal class. The **extDim** and **extTw** inputs handle dimensions, central characters and twists. Real norm twists are unconditional. For a general character twist the proof chooses a presenting multisegment, uses the goodness of the support on each head and twists the corresponding individual parameters. The theorem asserts the absolute central-character comparison on all classes and the full central-character identity on good support. See `LocalCentralUnits/RecTExists.lean:27-57` and `RcTwOfSegments.lean:36-57`.

Duality and injectivity on good support enter as the separate **extDual** and **extInj** inputs. For the depth conclusion, consider a wild inertia element. At a principal-congruence fixed vector, the depth property descends to the cuspidals in the support. On a good constituent of rank $j$, the characteristic polynomial on wild inertia is $(X-1)^j$. The product formula for the support and additivity of its ranks give $(X-1)^{\dim r_c(x)}$. The tameness criterion in **extDepth** converts this identity to the claimed property. See `LocalCentralUnits/RecTExists.lean:39-44` and `ExtDepth.lean:11-34`.

## Returning the inertia polynomial

The type condition applies over a base completion continuously isomorphic to $\mathbb Q_p$. At the chosen place $w'$ upstairs the prime is unramified, expressed by $p\notin (w')^2$, and the residue field has cardinality $p^n$ for $n>0$. One uses a surjective residue map with kernel $w'$, a regular residue-field character and an inflated cuspidal type with the prescribed elliptic character values. These are hypotheses of **TypeLaw**, `NonGaloisDescent/RecTExists.lean:34-52`. The source uses **typeIff** to pass between a law expressed by a residue map on inertia and the law using an Artin unit over that extension. In the direction used here it constructs the extension record and its reduction map, restricts inertia to the base, and identifies the resulting residue unit with the prescribed one. This is why matching the element in the exponent is part of the argument. See `TypeIff.lean:46-57`.

Finally inflation of the stipulated type gives cuspidality of the class in question. Replace $r_c(x)$ by $r(x)$ at precisely this class. The cuspidal type law supplies zero monodromy and the characteristic polynomial with roots $\vartheta(z^{p^j})$ for $0\le j<n$ at the specified inertia element. Both clauses concern the same parameter. This last specialization is **recTExists**, `LocalCentralUnits/RecTExists.lean:58-66`.
