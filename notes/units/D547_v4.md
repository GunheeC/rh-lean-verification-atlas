Assume a fixed host satisfying its frame axioms, a real scale $x$, the explicit-formula datum $\mathrm{EAtH}(\Lambda,H,TT,x,\varepsilon,C_E)$, and a logarithmic-derivative function $\ell$ compatible with the matched primitive characters. Here $TT$ is the set of selected places and $n_T=|TT|$.

Put $E_0=(\mathbb Z/2)^2$, $s_0=((1,0),0)$, $e_1(t)=s_0+(0,t)$ and $\mathcal A=\{\xi\in\widehat{E_0\times H}:\xi(0,A_0)=1\}$. The character sum at $y$ is denoted by $G_\xi(y)$. Write $K'$ for the kept subset, $o=\Lambda.o$, $c=(3o+1)^{-1}$, $M=4o$, $K_o=\sum_{\psi\in\Lambda.\mathrm{KEPT}}|(L'/L)(1,\psi)|$ with the stated convention for the logarithmic derivative at one.

Using weight one on characters trivial on $H$ and $c$ on the others, the finite host sum equals $\mathcal G_\Lambda(y)$ at every real $y$.

Let $P$ be the host modulus and let $\mathrm{art}:(\mathbb Z/P)^\times \to\phi.\mathrm{GG}$ be its surjective Artin map. The frame isomorphism $\iota_E:\phi.\mathrm{GG}\to E_0\times H$ sends $((a,b),m)$ to $(\sigma(b),\sigma(a),\iota^{-1}m)$, where $\sigma(+1)=0$, $\sigma(-1)=1$. Its inverse decodes the two signs in reverse order and uses $\iota$ on the last coordinate. Hence $\theta_\xi=\xi\circ\iota_E$ runs once through the host characters. Let $p_\xi$ be the primitive character inducing $\theta_\xi\circ\mathrm{art}$ on units. If $p_\xi=p_{\xi'}$, inducing both to $P$ gives equal unit values. Surjectivity first of $\mathrm{art}$ and then of $\iota_E$ gives $\xi=\xi'$. The zero additive character corresponds to the principal primitive character $\mathbf1$. By definition $K'=\{\xi:p_\xi\in\Lambda.\mathrm{KEPT}\}$. The kept clause of $\mathrm{EAtH}$ represents each member of $\Lambda.\mathrm{KEPT}$ by a host character trivial on $\tau_1(A_0)$ and on $\mathrm{iod}$. Consequently $K'\to\Lambda.\mathrm{KEPT}$ is bijective. By injectivity these two triviality properties hold for every $\theta_\xi$ with $\xi\in K'$. In additive coordinates they read $\xi(0,A_0)=1$ and $\xi(s_0)=1$. See `toE`, `recH` (`PrimitiveCosets/RecH.lean:10–23`), `chr_bij`, `prim_inj` and `EAtH` (`ChrBij.lean:25–29`, `PrimInj.lean:6–27`, `ExplicitFormulaErrorOfHostConditions.lean:144–151`).

We check that the two prime sums use the same character even at primes dividing $P$. For a prime power $p^i$ with $i>0$, the local factor in `primVal` is the character average on $i\operatorname{Frob}_p+I_p$. If $\xi$ is nontrivial on $I_p$, translation by an element with value different from one annihilates the average. In this case $p$ divides the conductor of $p_\xi$ and its Dirichlet value is zero. Indeed, by the Chinese remainder theorem the kernel of reduction to the prime-to-$p$ part of $P$ is the local factor $(\mathbb Z/p^{v_p(P)}\mathbb Z)^\times$, and its image is $I_p$. Killing that kernel is equivalent to factoring the character through the prime-to-$p$ modulus. If it is killed, the average is $\xi(\operatorname{Frob}_p)^i$; the Frobenius unit is congruent to $p$ modulo that prime-to-$p$ modulus and hence modulo the conductor. Thus in both cases the factor is $p_\xi(p)^i$. Products over prime powers give equality at every positive integer. For $\xi\ne0$, compatibility of $\ell$ now gives $\ell(\xi)=(L'/L)(1,p_\xi)$ in the convention of the manuscript. The two sums run over $1\le n\le\lfloor y\rfloor$ and $(y/n-1)_+=y/n-1$ on this range; it is empty for $y<1$. The principal terms have the same explicit correction. Thus $G_\xi(y)=g_{p_\xi}(y)$ for every real $y$, with $g_{\mathbf1}=G$ the level's principal function. See `primitive_value_primitive` and `cosetMean_eq_primitive` (`ChainSlopeWindows/GaloisMapComplexConjugation.lean:86–96,129–150,209–245`), `prim_read` and `Gz_eq` (`PrimitiveCosets/PrimRead.lean:25–97`, `PrimitiveCosets/GzEq.lean:6–50`).

For a kept $\xi$ trivial on all of $H$, the host character kills the $H$ factor and $\mathrm{iod}=((1,-1),1)$. It factors through the first sign. Its value at $((-1,1),1)$ has square one, so it is the trivial character or the sign character $\phi.\mathrm{psC}$. Both restrictions are conversely trivial on $H$. The first has level weight one. If the second is kept, the exceptional-character clause of $\mathrm{EAtH}$ identifies its primitive character with $\Lambda.\mathrm{psK}$ and gives it weight one: an absent $\mathrm{psK}$ explicitly precludes keeping $\phi.\mathrm{psC}$. Every other kept character has nontrivial $H$ restriction and is neither principal nor exceptional by injectivity, so both weights are $c$. Reindex the weighted sum over $K'$ by $\xi\mapsto p_\xi$, then separate $\mathbf1$ from the remaining terms. This proves $G_m^A(y)=\mathcal G_\Lambda(y)$, D547 (`gmA_eq_triv`, `gmA_eq`, `PrimitiveCosets/GmAEq.lean:16–59,61–149`).
