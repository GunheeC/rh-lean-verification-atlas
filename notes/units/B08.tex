\begin{proposition}[A one-scale bound at a host]\label{sub:B08}
For a level $\mathcal L$, fixed real $e_D,e_L,K,c_{HH},e_K,C$ and
$\varepsilon>0$ there exists $x_1$ such that, for every $x\ge x_1$,
frame, host on that frame and finite test set $TT$, the following two
conditions imply
\[
       \mathcal G_{\mathcal L}(x)\le x^{1/2+(e_D+e_L+7)\varepsilon}.
\]
First, the host and level have the same order $o$, represent every kept
character in the positive family, and agree on the distinguished
quadratic character's exceptional status. Each translate of the subgroup
$A_0$ has a share of $TT$ within $x^{-1+\varepsilon}$ of its target
($4/(3o+1)$ for $A_0$ and $3/(3o+1)$ otherwise, in the equivalent
unnormalised counting inequality). For the positive-family characters
outside the kept set and all negative-family characters the bounds are
$|G_\chi(x)|\le x^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ and $|(L'/L)(1,\chi)|\le C\log x$.

Second, there are finite unit and word data, with nonempty word fibres,
a map onto every element of $A_0$, and arithmetic coefficients whose
nontrivial character logarithmic derivatives agree with the host's.
They identify the lattice top and place indices with $TT$ and the
index-two subgroup, and satisfy the lattice hypotheses, error-window
and $B_0,N_c$ bounds in \hyperref[sub:B10]{B10} and the size, thin,
cost and remaining-size conditions in \hyperref[sub:B09]{B09}, with
$T_2\subset A_0$. The scale and exponent of the lattice data are
$x,\varepsilon$; their division, loss and height exponents are bounded
above by $e_D,e_L,c_{HH}$ respectively.
\end{proposition}\subnode{B08}
The threshold precedes the frame, the host and $TT$.
