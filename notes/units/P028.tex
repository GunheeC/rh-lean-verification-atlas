\begin{theorem}[The lower bound from integral derivations]\label{pn:P028}
Let \(O\) be a discrete valuation ring with finite residue field, \(A\)
an algebra graded by an abelian group, \(x:A\to O\) a point vanishing on
\(A_j\), and \(F_i\in A_j\). Fix \(0\ne c\in O\). Suppose an
additive injection \(D:M\to\operatorname{Hom}_O(A,O/(c))\) kills all
\(F_i\) at every value. Suppose each \(D(\mu)\) is the reduction of an
\(O\)-valued point derivation of weight \(j\) at \(x\). With the
saturated lattice as above,
\[
 \operatorname{length}_O M \le
 \operatorname{length}_O(L_j/\langle\overline F_i\rangle).
\]
\end{theorem}\nodestatus{P028}
\begin{proof}
A point derivation kills \((\ker x)^2\); its integral lift kills the
saturation because \(O\) is torsion-free. Every \(D(\mu)\) therefore
gives a functional on the displayed quotient \(Q\). The map to
\(\operatorname{Hom}_O(Q,O/(c))\) is injective: its values determine
\(D(\mu)\) in degree \(j\), and all other homogeneous pieces vanish
by the weight condition. If \(Q\) has infinite length the inequality
is immediate. Otherwise induction on a composition series bounds
the length of the dual by \(\operatorname{length}Q\), since the
submodule of \(O/(c)\) killed by the maximal ideal has length at most
one. Every finite-length module has cardinality equal to the residue
cardinality raised to its length. Comparing cardinalities under the
additive injection proves the result without assuming \(O\)-linearity
of \(D\). This is the second deduction of Blueprint Z1.4~\bploc{sec:P-z14-full}.
\end{proof}
