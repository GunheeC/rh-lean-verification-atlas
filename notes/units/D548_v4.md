Assume a fixed host satisfying its frame axioms, $x>0$, the explicit-formula datum $\mathrm{EAtH}(\Lambda,H,TT,x,\varepsilon,C_E)$, and a logarithmic-derivative function $\ell$ compatible with the matched primitive characters. Here $TT$ is the set of selected places and $n_T=|TT|$.

Put $E_0=(\mathbb Z/2)^2$, $s_0=((1,0),0)$, $e_1(t)=s_0+(0,t)$ and $\mathcal A=\{\xi\in\widehat{E_0\times H}:\xi(0,A_0)=1\}$. The character sum at $y$ is denoted by $G_\xi(y)$. Write $K'$ for the kept subset, $o=\Lambda.o$, $c=(3o+1)^{-1}$, $M=4o$, $K_o=\sum_{\psi\in\Lambda.\mathrm{KEPT}}|(L'/L)(1,\psi)|$ with the stated convention for the logarithmic derivative at one.

The Artin map is onto, so two host characters inducing the same primitive character coincide on every unit of the frame. The kept clause represents each primitive member of the level by a character trivial on $0\times A_0$ and on $s_0$. Thus these restrictions hold for each kept representative. The dictionary and the equality of character values, including at ramified primes, are proved in [the kept-character identification](D547_v4.md).

We have $0\in K'$ since $\mathbf1\in\Lambda.\mathrm{KEPT}$, and the two required triviality assertions follow from the kept clause. A character trivial on $0\times A_0$ factors uniquely through $E_0\times H/A_0$: it is constant on each fibre, and the quotient map is onto. The latter group has $4[H:A_0]=4o$ characters (a cyclic factor of order $d$ has exactly the $d$ choices of a $d$th root of unity). Thus $|\mathcal A|=M$. Fix a kept $\xi$ nontrivial on $H$, and let $\alpha$ be its restriction on $H/A_0$. Set $n_v=|TT\cap v|$ for each coset $v$. The shares hypothesis reads

$$

 n_v=(3+\mathbf1_{v=0})c n_T+e_v,\qquad
 |e_v|\le x^{-1+\varepsilon}n_T .

$$

Translation by a coset with $\alpha(v)\ne1$ gives $\sum_v\alpha(v)=0$. Multiplication by $\alpha(v)$ and summation give $\sum_{t\in TT}\xi(0,t)-cn_T=\sum_v\alpha(v)e_v$, of modulus at most $o x^{-1+\varepsilon}n_T\le M x^{-1+\varepsilon}n_T$. Each nonzero kept $\xi$ has $|\ell(\xi)|\le K_o$ because its norm is one nonnegative summand in the defining sum for $K_o$. For $\xi\in\mathcal A\setminus K'$ the value of $\theta_\xi$ at $\mathrm{iod}$ is $+1$ or $-1$ since $\mathrm{iod}^2=1$. In the first case it belongs to the unkept positive-sign class and in the second to the negative-sign class. These are the two branches of the host's bounded set. Since $\xi\ne0$, its matched logarithmic derivative therefore has modulus at most $C_E\log x$ by the bounded derivative clause.
