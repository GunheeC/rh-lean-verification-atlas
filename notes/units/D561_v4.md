## A8 · Prime-power balance

**Prime powers in one column.**

Write $U=(\mathbb Z/2)^2\times H$ and $\Phi=\{g=((g_1,g_2),h):g_1=0\}$. Thus $|U|=2f$ with $f=|\Phi|=2|H|$. The character $\chi_C(g)=(-1)^{g_1}$ distinguishes the two cosets of $\Phi$. For $t\in H$ set $P_t(g)=((1,1),0)+g+((0,0),t)$. Translation by this fixed element interchanges $\Phi$ and its complement. The data at a prime $p$ are a subgroup $I_p\le U$ and an element $F_p\in U$. Write $0\in\widehat U$ for the character identically one and $[z]_+=\max(z,0)$ for real $z$. The host hypothesis here is `Deduction.CH.HostFactsS` (D014): a finite set $S$ contains $2,q_1$, and $d$ is a prime outside $S$. For primes $p\notin S\cup\{d\}$, $I_p=\{0\}$; for $p\in S$, $I_p\subset\Phi$ and $F_p\in\Phi$; and $I_d=\{0,((1,0),0)\}$. `DedekindLFunctions/SplitInertia.lean:14–22`; `InPhi`, `cconj`, `topPl`, `ThickAverageLoss/PartnersToFactorsAt.lean:20–29`; `partner`, `chC`, `ArithData`, `SizeWindowBrackets/FirstPrimeNeSecondPrime.lean:19–42`.

**Exact prime and character balance**

Take the host just specified, any function $\ell:\widehat U\to\mathbb C$, $a>0$, $t\in H$, a width function $w:U\to\mathbb R$ positive on $\Phi$, and $N\in\mathbb N$ with $a/w(\rho)\le N$ for every $\rho\in\Phi$. Put $W_g=w(P_t(g))$ for $g\notin\Phi$ and $h(v,g)=[a/v-W_g]_+$. Here $p$ is called inert if it is prime, $p\notin\{2,q_1,d\}$ and $F_p\notin\Phi$. Then, as an identity in $\mathbb C$, $\mathrm{ASK}-\mathrm{MAIN}=\mathrm{De}$, where the three quantities are

$$

\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{ASK}={}&
 2\sum_{\substack{p\le N\\p\ {\rm inert}}}
  (\log p)\!\sum_{\substack{1\le i\le N\\iF_p\notin\Phi}}h(p^i,iF_p)
 + (\log d)\sum_{i=1}^N
  \sum_{\substack{g\notin\Phi\\g-iF_d\in I_d}} h(d^i,g),\\
 \mathrm{MAIN}={}& a\left(\log a-1-\gamma+\ell(\chi_C)
           -\frac1f\sum_{\rho\in\Phi}\log w(\rho)\right),\\
 \mathrm{De}={}&\frac1f\sum_{g\notin\Phi}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat U}
      \overline{\xi(g)}\,W_g G_\xi(a/W_g).
\end{aligned}

$$

Here $G_\xi$ is the centered character sum defined in the proof and $\gamma$ is Euler's constant.

`IsInert`, `askP`, `askD`, `ASK`, `MAIN`, `De`, `SizeWindowBrackets/FirstPrimeNeSecondPrime.lean:72–96`; `Deduction.ch_balance`, `LandauTheorems/ChBalance.lean:200–260`.

**Proof.**

For $n\ge1$ let $v_\xi(n)$ be the product over $p\mid n$ of the means of $\xi$ on $\operatorname{ord}_p(n)F_p+I_p$. The empty product is one, and $v_0(n)=1$. At a prime power $p^i$, $i\ge1$, the product has just one factor. Finite Fourier orthogonality thus gives

$$

 \frac1f\sum_{\xi\in\widehat U}\overline{\xi(g)}v_\xi(p^i)
 =\frac{1}{f|I_p|}\sum_{z\in I_p}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat U}
                         \xi(iF_p+z-g)
 =\frac{2}{|I_p|}\,\mathbf 1_{g-iF_p\in I_p}.

$$

Indeed the inner sum at zero is $|U|$: decompose $U$ into cyclic factors, on each of which the characters are the roots of unity of the corresponding order. At a nonzero element one of the cyclic geometric sums vanishes. At most one $z$ has $iF_p+z-g=0$. This explains both the inertia divisor and the factor $2$. See `cosetMean`, `primVal`, `FirstPrimeNeSecondPrime.lean:61–66`; `orthogonality_ramified`, `ChainSlopeWindows/StronglyIrreducible.lean:311–317`.

Consider the finite Fourier sum

$$

 J=\frac1f\sum_{g\notin\Phi}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat U}\overline{\xi(g)}
       \sum_{n=1}^N\Lambda(n)v_\xi(n)h(n,g).

$$

Only prime powers $n=p^i$ contribute, with $\Lambda(n)=\log p$. Since $P_t(g)\in\Phi$, $a/W_g\le N$. Thus $h(p^i,g)=0$ whenever $p^i>N$, and one can sum over $p\le N$ and $1\le i\le N$. Evaluate the character sum using the preceding identity.

- For $p\in S$, both $F_p$ and $I_p$ lie in $\Phi$, so no $g\notin\Phi$ contributes. In particular $F_p\in\Phi$ excludes inertness, and the first term of ASK also vanishes at this prime (`ask` decomposition, `ChBalance.lean:163–167`).

- For $p\notin S\cup\{d\}$, only $g=iF_p$ can contribute, with coefficient $2$. If $F_p\in\Phi$, all its multiples lie in $\Phi$. Otherwise $p$ is inert (since $2,q_1\in S$) and precisely the exponents with $iF_p\notin\Phi$ give the first term of $\mathrm{ASK}$.

- For $p=d$, the coefficient on $iF_d+I_d$ is $2/2=1$. Intersect this coset with $U\setminus\Phi$ to get the second term of $\mathrm{ASK}$. If $d>N$, every $d^i>N$ and the written term vanishes.

Hence $J=\mathrm{ASK}$. The split, unramified and ramified sums and their recombination are `CharacterBalance.lsplit`, `lunram`, `ld` and `askExp`, `LandauTheorems/ChBalance.lean:42–196`.

Evaluate $J$ in the other order. Put

$$

 L_\xi(y)=\sum_{1\le n\le y}\Lambda(n)v_\xi(n)(y/n-1).

$$

Then $G_0(y)=L_0(y)-y\log y+(1+\gamma)y$ and, for $\xi\ne0$, $G_\xi(y)=L_\xi(y)+y\ell(\xi)$. Fix $g\notin\Phi$, put $W=W_g$ and $y=a/W$. Since $0<y\le N$, the inner $n$-sum is $W L_\xi(y)$: on $n\le y$ the factor is $W(y/n-1)$, and on $n>y$ it vanishes. Consequently this sum equals $WG_\xi(y)$ plus the correction

$$

 \begin{cases}
 a\log a-a\log W-(1+\gamma)a,&\xi=0,\\
 -a\ell(\xi),&\xi\ne0.
 \end{cases}

$$

These are `GPsi`, `FirstPrimeNeSecondPrime.lean:68–70`, `Gz`, `Formalism/StatementOC.lean:11–13`, and `sum_cut`, `inner_eq`, `ChainSlopeWindows/StronglyIrreducible.lean:619–686`.

The $WG_\xi(y)$ terms give $\mathrm{De}$. For $\xi=0$, the map $g\mapsto P_t(g)$ is a bijection onto $\Phi$ with inverse $\rho\mapsto\rho-((1,1),0)-((0,0),t)$. Its averaged correction is $a\log a-(1+\gamma)a-(a/f)\sum_{\rho\in\Phi}\log w(\rho)$. For the nontrivial characters, use $\mathbf 1_{g\notin\Phi}=(1-\chi_C(g))/2$. Character orthogonality gives

$$

 \frac1f\sum_{g\notin\Phi}\overline{\xi(g)}
  =\mathbf 1_{\xi=0}-\mathbf 1_{\xi=\chi_C}.

$$

Thus the nontrivial-character correction is $+a\ell(\chi_C)$, with the plus sign coming from the two minus signs. Together the corrections give exactly $\mathrm{MAIN}$. Comparing $J=\mathrm{De}+\mathrm{MAIN}$ with $J=\mathrm{ASK}$ proves the identity. See `partner_bijection`, `colour_sum`, `ChainSlopeWindows/StronglyIrreducible.lean:153–212`, and `ch_balance`, `LandauTheorems/ChBalance.lean:206–260`.

## A10 · Fixed-width thick places

**The thick part of the place sum at fixed width**

Write $g=((\alpha,\beta),h)\in E_0\times H$ and $\Phi=\{g:\alpha=0\}$. Let

$$

 \begin{gathered}
 c_{\rm conj}=((1,1),0),\qquad
 \operatorname{topPl}(t)=((0,0),t),\qquad
 \operatorname{thickPl}(s)=((0,1),s),\\
 P_t(g)=c_{\rm conj}+g+\operatorname{topPl}(t)
       =((1+\alpha,1+\beta),h+t).
 \end{gathered}

$$

This $P_t$ is `partner`. The condition `IsThickAt`$(T_2,u,\rho)$ means $\rho=\operatorname{thickPl}(s+u)$ for some $s\in T_2$. Fix $a=a_t$, $b>0$, and a column with weights $w$. In the Fourier sum $\mathrm{De}$ retain the terms with thick partners only and replace $w(P_t(g))$ by $b$ in both occurrences. The resulting complex sum is

$$

 T(u,t)=\frac{b}{|\Phi|}\sum_{s\in T_2}
      \sum_{\xi\in\widehat{E_0\times H}}
        \xi(-e_1(s+u-t))\,G_\xi(a/b).

$$

**Proof.**

The first sign of $g+\operatorname{topPl}(t)$ is $\alpha$, so the translated complement of $\Phi$ is exactly the original complement. There $\alpha=1$ and $P_t(g)=((0,1+\beta),h+t)$ belongs to $\Phi$. Finite character values have modulus one, hence $\overline{\xi(g)}=\xi(-g)$. Inserting these two equalities in the definition of $\mathrm{De}$ gives

$$

 \Re\mathrm{De}=\Re\left(\frac{1}{|\Phi|}
 \sum_{g+\operatorname{topPl}(t)\notin\Phi}\sum_\xi
 \xi(-g) w(P_t(g))\,G_\xi(a/w(P_t(g)))\right).

$$

This is the assertion `De_re`: the associated cyclic host has the same character functions, term by term. Now $P_t(g)$ is thick exactly when $\beta=0$ and $h=s+u-t$ for some $s\in T_2$. That $s=h-u+t$ is unique, and the inverse is $g=e_1(s+u-t)$. Reindexing by this bijection and inserting the constant width $b$ gives the claimed complex equality before taking any real parts. The thin contribution disappears in this frozen sum; comparison with the original $\mathrm{De}$ also incurs the change of width at each thick place, as estimated in the enclosing moving-width argument. `InPhi`, `topPl`, `thickPl`, `IsThickAt` and `balTop`, `ThickAverageLoss/PartnersToFactorsAt.lean:20–29,62–65,97`; `partner`, `De`, `SizeWindowBrackets/FirstPrimeNeSecondPrime.lean:19,93–96`; `De_re`, `GC_host`, `ChainSlopeWindows/LipschitzWeightAndMinimum.lean:147–169`; `Th`, `th_le`, `LandauTheorems/ChCore.lean:16–20,75–104`.
