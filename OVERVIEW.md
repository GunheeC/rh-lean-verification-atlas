## What an analytic number theorist can test first

Assuming V, F, Green and the listed local and intermediate inputs, consider the analytic step. For each power of $\psi$, take the primitive character that induces it. These characters form its cyclic level $\Lambda$ and carry specified weights. Write $\mathcal G_\Lambda(x)$ for its real smoothed prime sum. Suppose that for every starting point there is a larger $Y$ such that $\mathcal G_\Lambda(x)\le x^\theta$ throughout $[Y,Y^{3/2}]$, for fixed $1/2<\theta<1$. In the open strip, each zero of a level character gives a pole of the transformed sum. Positive level weights prevent cancellation of the weighted zero multiplicities. The Landau window theorem bounds the real parts of these poles by $\theta$. For $\Re s\ge1$ the internal level criterion also uses classical nonvanishing. It treats the modulus-one character using the formal zeta convention. The final statement for characters of arbitrary nonzero modulus excludes the principal character at $s=1$. The one-sided bound on arbitrarily distant *intervals* is the analytic input. A bound just at the endpoints would leave the interior uncontrolled. Start at P023 to inspect the passage from character zeros to pole data, then at P022 for the window theorem itself. ## Where the half power enters

Assuming the same global, local and intermediate inputs, at a fixed scale $x$, the lattice estimate bounds the excess of a weighted prime-power count over its main term. Finite character orthogonality evaluates the same sum on inertia cosets. If $U$ is the finite character host and $\Phi$ its index-two subgroup, the Fourier sum with normalization $1/|\Phi|$ contributes $2/|I_p|$ when the specified Frobenius coset contains the group element, and zero otherwise. Trivial inertia therefore contributes a factor two. The distinguished ramified prime has inertia of order two and contributes a factor one. The principal and sign-character corrections give the exact identity $\mathrm{ASK}-\mathrm{MAIN}=\mathrm{De}$. This identifies the quantity bounded by the lattice argument with one that can be averaged over characters. [Host inertia](units/D014.md) · [Lattice estimate](units/B10.md) · [Character average](units/B09.md)

Assuming V, F, Green and the local and intermediate inputs, normalize the lattice error and average over characters. On the chosen family the resulting estimate is
$$
\mathcal G_\Lambda(x)\le x^{1/2+(e_D+e_L+7)\varepsilon}.
$$
Here $e_D$ and $e_L$ are the exponents for the division and loss bounds on the chosen family. The local derivation fixes that family and its constants before taking the tail. [Normalization](units/B09.md) · [Passage to the level](units/B08.md) · [Family inequality](units/P017.md)

## Why two arithmetic inputs are needed

**Assume V for the Selmer bound.** For a primitive character, a function choosing a dyadic exponent within the prescribed bounds, and $0<\varepsilon<1/52$, choose the constants $J\ge1$ and $X_0$ given by V. At each $X\ge X_0$, every admissible prime pair has a common interval $[Y,Y^2]$ with $X^{1/J}\le Y$ and $Y^2\le X$. At each $x$ in it put $t=\lfloor x^{401}\rfloor$. For the allowed fields and local data, write $h_\ell(j)$ for the length of the localization kernel at prime $\ell$ and twist $j$. V gives finiteness and, for every finite set $S$ of primes,
$$
\sum_{\ell\in S}\log\ell\sum_{1\le j<t}h_\ell(j)\le \exp(x^\varepsilon/2).
$$
There is no complete human-readable derivation of V in this atlas. With V, the rank bound and the hypotheses for the host in list B, the construction chooses the auxiliary primes, field, frame and ranks and passes to a $3/2$-window. It supplies the bound at the chosen host's actual rank together with its explicit-formula estimate. Its prime, field and rank implications form list B. [Selmer window](units/P013.md) · [Supply of a host](units/B07.md)

**Assume F for a common selection.** F asks for one record containing a class of families and a compatible selection on the definite unitary side. Its norm comparison, called the closed link, applies to each eligible family beyond the stated threshold. In the subsequent deduction the selected data let the divisibility comparison use V’s length bound for that same family. Together with Green's finite-field character input and the geometric comparisons in list C, a separate slope estimate then yields the hypothesis needed to tie the chain. There is no complete human-readable derivation of F in this atlas. This is a point of entry for readers interested in heights or theta periods. V’s exponential bound alone gives no power bound for the prime sum. Under the additional division and slope comparisons on the same selected family, the pointwise lattice inequality bounds a normalized error, and the character average yields the half-power exponent. This overview does not derive those comparisons. [Exact inputs and Lean sources](units/V4-COND.md) · [Pointwise lattice bound](units/P021.md)

Assuming all these additional inputs as well as V, F, Green and lists A–C, the calculation gives $e_D=2$ and $e_L=0$. For a putative zero with real part $\beta>1/2$ away from the principal pole, choose $1/2<\theta<\min(1,\beta)$ and then an admissible $\varepsilon$ with $9\varepsilon<\theta-1/2$. For this $\varepsilon$ the host and common-family construction supplies a window set containing intervals $[Y,Y^{3/2}]$ with arbitrarily large $Y$. P017 bounds the prime sum on the tail of that set. Thus for every requested starting point an entire interval lies beyond both it and the bound’s threshold, as required by the level criterion P018. This contradicts the putative zero. Change of conductor and the functional equation give the five indicated zeta and Dirichlet formulations, with the principal-character exception at $s=1$.
