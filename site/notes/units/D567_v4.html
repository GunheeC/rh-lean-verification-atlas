How the lattice error fits the arithmetic bracket.

The lattice estimate uses the following bracket and fixed threshold. Write

$$ E=n_Ta(1+m^2/k)+m(B_0+N_c),\qquad K_* =200(1+K)^2(1+c_{HH})(1+e_K). $$

These are ErrText and KBig (ErrorFormsWeakWindowsFalse.lean:16–21).

Per-unit error, D567

For a finite lattice record with nonempty word fibers, assume the size conditions ErrWindows and $\log x,K_*\le x^\varepsilon$. Then for every unit $u$,

$$ \mathrm{ErrX5at}(u)\le x^{(e_{\rm Div}+e_{\rm Loss}+3)\varepsilon} E . $$

The size conditions are $x,b,n_T\ge1$, $0<\varepsilon\le1$, $xb\le a$, $m\le x^{1/2+\varepsilon/2}$, $xm\le k$, $e_K\ge0$, $1\le k\le x^{e_K}$, $k\le a$, $0\le L,L_a\le2x^\varepsilon$, $0\le kb'\le a$, $0\le X''\le a$, $0\le a_t\le2a$, $B_0,N_c\ge0$, $\log|W(u)|\le(k-3)\log2$, $\bar\delta\le1/2$, $\bar\eta,\bar e\ge0$. Each of $c_{\rm Div},c_E,c_H,c_{\rm Count},c_{\rm Slope},c_{\rm Sl1},c_{\rm Loss}$ is in $[0,K]$ and $e_{\rm Div},e_{\rm Loss},c_{HH}\ge0$. The final window condition bounds $(\bar\eta+4\sqrt{\bar e})x^{c_{HH}}b\le Kx^\varepsilon n_Ta$. See ErrWindows, LargeX (ErrorFormsWeakWindowsFalse.lean:23–52).

Proof.

Set $y=x^\varepsilon$, $d=x^{e_{\rm Div}\varepsilon}$, $z=x^{e_{\rm Loss}\varepsilon}$, all at least one. Since $m\le x$, we have $m\le k$ and $mb\le a$. Each of $1,b,a,n_Ta,n_Tam^2/k,mB_0,m(B_0+N_c)$ is at most $E$. Put $A=\sum_t a_t\le2n_Ta$. The two logarithms obey $\log k\le e_Ky$ and $\log(1+x^{c_{HH}})\le(1+c_{HH})y$ since $x^{c_{HH}}\ge1$, $\log 2\le1$ and $\log x\le y$. For each word fiber, $0\le\log|W(u)|\le k\le a$.

In the formula ErrX5at (QuotientSlopeBoundsWith.lean:37–54) divide the terms outside the final fraction by $E$. The counting term is at most $4Ky$, since $A L_a(1+m^2/k)\le4y E$. The two divisor terms cost $2Ky$ and $Kd$. The exponent comparison costs $K$ and the slope term costs $4Ky$ since $1+m/k\le2$. The word logarithm costs at most $1$. The prime loss satisfies $Lo_P/E\le Kz$ since $n_Tmb\le n_Ta$. The mean-loss bound is

$$ M/E\le K^2 z+4K^2y+K+1 . $$

Indeed its count term contains $n_Tm(a/k+b')mL\le4n_Tam^2y/k$. Its two other additive terms are $c_{\rm Sl1}X''$ and $1$. Adding the eight costs yields at most $20(1+K)^2ydz$.

The numerator of the fraction also uses $\bar U=K_3+M$. From the record's definition of $V_{\rm var}$,

$$ V_{\rm var}/E\le 2K+K(2y+1)+2Ke_Ky . $$

The first bound is for $2mc_EB_0$, the second for $c_H(n_TaL_a+mB_0)$, and the last for $Ac_{\rm Count}\log k$. The two extra terms in $K_3/E$ cost at most $6Ky$ and $K$. Consequently $(\bar U+b)/E\le14(1+K)^2(1+e_K)yz$. All upper bounds are multiplied by nonnegative coefficients: $(1-\bar\delta)^{-1}\in(0,2]$ and $\log(1+x^{c_{HH}})\ge0$. Substitution of these bounds and of the final window condition gives

$$ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{ErrX5at}(u)/E &\le20(1+K)^2ydz +112(1+K)^2(1+e_K)(1+c_{HH})y^2z+2Ky\\ &\le K_*y^2dz\le y^3dz . \end{aligned} $$

Here $20+112+2\le200$ and the last inequality uses $K_*\le y$. Since $E\ge1$, multiplication by $E$ proves the claim. This is a direct estimate of the same explicit polynomial as LChain.errX5at_le (ChainSlopeWindows/PChain.lean:613–672).

The common large-scale threshold yields the two numerical assumptions on $\log x$ and $K_*$.
