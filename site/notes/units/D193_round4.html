D193 · Extending the generic isogeny to a normal flat model

The generic Hecke correspondence supplies two maps and a universal isogeny. Its normalization has to carry extensions of both maps and of that same isogeny, retain the required type, and preserve the universal property on the given open. UpperTheory.Gal.crystallineComparison.Mod.esNorm_link (QuotObjLink.lean:10–10).

Let $p$ be prime and $R$ a characteristic-zero discrete valuation domain. Fix the datum $D$, a base over $R$, $N\ge3$ coprime to $p$, and assume $p$ lies in the maximal ideal of $R$. For an integer $r$, a maximal ideal $w$ containing uIdeal D r, and $j\le D.n$, take $c=p^e$ with $e>0$ whenever $j>0$. Assume $D.m=1$. Every level-$N$ model $M$ admits a scheme $Y$, maps $s,t:Y\to M.X$, and two objects with maps to the universal object along $s,t$. There is a morphism $\psi$ of their abelian objects. Both maps are finite, have the same composite to the base, and that composite is flat. Every stalk of $Y$ is integrally closed. The same $\psi$ has IsPIsog c and IsTj r w j, and the objects satisfy Univ r w j c over $M$. UpperTheory.Gal.crystallineComparison.Mod.EsNorm (Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/AnnihilatorModels/FrobKill.lean:125–126).

Take the generic objects as one system

The interface first takes the generic correspondence supplied by the in-repo theorem UpperTheory.Cb.exists_heckeCorrespondence_on_integral_shimuraModel. Its data include the generic scheme and both source and target maps, identifications of the pulled objects, the isogeny with its prescribed type, and a uniqueness property. Fix the moduli data, prime, level, discrete parameters and model before taking any of these objects. The normal-model supplier takes this whole system as a premise. Extending two generic maps independently and only afterwards choosing an isogeny would lose the commutation condition that appears later. UpperTheory.Gal.crystallineComparison.Mod.esGen_link (QuotObjLink.lean:9–9). UpperTheory.Gal.crystallineComparison.Mod.esNorm_link (QuotObjLink.lean:10–10). UpperTheory.Gal.crystallineComparison.Mod.esNorm_of (MEsNorm.lean:16–43).

Normalize over the source and extend the other map

The first construction produces a finite normal model over the source with its open immersion, flatness, local noetherian information and extension properties. The target map is then extended to this particular model, using the original generic target map and both sets of generic identifications. The argument supplies reducedness from the generic and flat model data. At each stalk it combines reducedness with the supplied normality to obtain a domain. This last step is used explicitly when extending the isogeny; flatness alone is not the recorded input. UpperTheory.Gal.crystallineComparison.Mod.esNorm_of (MEsNorm.lean:16–43).

Transfer the generic isogeny and its type

Pull back the universal objects along the two extended maps. After identifying their restrictions to the generic open, obtain the two comparison morphisms to the generic objects. The isogeny extension theorem gives an extended isogeny together with its commutative restriction square. A factorization theorem now transports the type condition along that square, and an open-immersion theorem transports existence and uniqueness from the original generic object. These are separate concluding clauses. For a reviewer checking the arithmetic of the isogeny, the important inputs are the actual comparisons and their commutation with the same isogeny. D174 gives a different comparison for an endomorphism detected by torsion points. UpperTheory.Gal.crystallineComparison.Mod.esNorm_of (MEsNorm.lean:16–43). UpperTheory.Gal.crystallineComparison.Mod.EsNorm (FrobKill.lean:125–126).

Distinguish the two restrictions on the output

Use a single symbol $i$ for the open immersion of the given generic correspondence into the chosen model $Y$. The construction obtains the source restriction along $i$, then explicitly identifies the target restriction before matching the two pulled-back objects. The new isogeny $\psi$ is compared with the old one through these two morphisms. In the last two lines invoking the generic type, the term uses both the equality on this square and the extensional property of the normal model. The uniqueness call additionally uses generic universality. These arguments identify where a check of uniqueness has to begin: matching a type on generic points already requires the chosen comparison square, while the uniqueness assertion quantifies over compatible extensions. Existence of a normal model for the source alone supplies neither of these two final clauses. UpperTheory.Gal.crystallineComparison.Mod.esNorm_of (MEsNorm.lean:16–43).
