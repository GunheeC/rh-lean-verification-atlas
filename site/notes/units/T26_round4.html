Claim and hypotheses

Assume an arithmetic intersection system, the three Hilbert--Samuel consequences T18, the bigness bound T17, the resolution statement ResKStatement, extension of homomorphisms of semiabelian schemes, the Fargues Hodge--Tate input, GAGA for sections, quotients by finite flat subgroups, the Zarhin splitting T22 and nef Riemann--Roch T23. Build the large-degree line

The first four inputs are the intersection system, the three Hilbert–Samuel consequences, arithmetic bigness and resolution. The proof extracts a successive-minima upper bound from the Hilbert–Samuel and bigness pair. Intersection existence and generic degree come from the system; resolution supplies point integrability. The remaining construction includes a change of chart coordinates, a dual lattice index, metric energy and a change of model. These are used together to produce a line of large degree. This step is a substantial geometric input to the rest of the argument. Supply one compatible tower and volume comparison

Extension of semiabelian homomorphisms, the Fargues Hodge–Tate input and GAGA for sections join the large-degree line to give the record called Given. The same data, supplemented by finite flat quotients and the Zarhin splitting, give a pair consisting of good-tower existence and the volume theorem. The tower construction extracts its model, geometric and analytic components from the same record. The last line invokes the final comparison, adding the point integrability obtained from resolution. The step pairs two propositions; later code must still specialize them to the relevant datum. Check the finite exceptional set

The volume estimate bounds the cardinality of the union of primes dividing twice the absolute discriminant with those dividing the level integer. The target supplies a restriction on the latter set. Split the union at the host threshold $n$. The small-prime part embeds in the first $n+1$ natural numbers. For the other part the host bad-prime estimate applies. The CM-type cardinality and the lower bound for the square of the signature sum absorb these estimates in the required bound. Apply the constant to this datum, using its geometric and level conditions, then substitute its root-discriminant and scale identities. The finite-set arithmetic is visible here.
