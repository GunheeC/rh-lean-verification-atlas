Outlined · pending_review. This estimate controls a Gaussian term at a fixed lattice unit by its main term and explicit losses. The varying slope quantity left on the right is a weighted mean on that unit. A later estimate bounds this mean using counts.

Claim and hypotheses

Under the ten size, division, comparison, counting, slope, height and finite-parameter assumptions, Ga(u) is at most Main(u)+log|W(u)|+K0v5+M plus (1−delBar) inverse times [4 log(1+x\textasciicircum{}cHH)(K3+M+b)+(etaBar+4sqrt(eBar))x\textasciicircum{}cHH b], where M is the weighted mean of Lo5.

$$\mathrm{Ga}(u)\le \mathrm{Main}(u)+\log|W_u|+K_{0,v5}+M+\frac{4\log(1+x^{c_{HH}})(K_3+M+b)+(\bar\eta+4\sqrt{\bar e})\,x^{c_{HH}}b}{1-\bar\delta}.$$

Insert the main term and compare the losses

The exact identity rewrites the relevant division expression as the main term plus an explicit counting cost. It needs positive $v$, positive $f$ and positive individual weights from the setting. The finite-parameter and height bounds license the slope inequality. The logarithmic loss uses $u_F\ge 0$, the bound on $u_F$, and a positive $x^{c_{HH}}$. A second loss is the product of the height with $\eta+4\sqrt e$. Its coefficient and the height are nonnegative. Replace first the coefficient by its uniform upper bound and then the height by $x^{c_{HH}}b$. Preserve the direction of reciprocal comparison

The hypotheses give positive denominators $1-\delta(u_0)$ and $1-\bar\delta$, with the latter no larger. Taking reciprocals bounds the local reciprocal above by the uniform one. Both original losses are nonnegative, so multiplying their sum by the larger reciprocal preserves an upper bound. The uniform reciprocal is also nonnegative, allowing replacement of the terms inside the brackets. Expanding $K_5$ and $K_0$ gives the claimed bound by collecting terms.
