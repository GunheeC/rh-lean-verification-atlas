The unramified fixed-space assertion

The first call supplies GlobalTheory.Hk3.HoweUnram. Work with the valued field, involution, uniformizer, Hermitian matrices, additive character, actions on the complex module and hypotheses on the local dual pair in the theorem. The test vector $s$ is nonzero and satisfies the stipulated invariance and generating-span hypotheses. The construction must give the fixed-vector span conclusion for both sides of the pair. This input is used together with the theta-local record and the convergent Siegel–Weil field in T09. GlobalTheory.Hk3.howeUnram_holds, RhoMemOfMkEq.lean:250–256; UpperTheory.Lit.functionalsSeparate_of_howeUnram_of_siegelWeil, FunctionalsSeparateOfHoweUnramOfSiegelWeil.lean:17–19.

  1. The one-sided calculation constructs an invariant span from translates of $s$. It tests its generators against the integral subgroup, commutes the two group actions and uses the prescribed fixed vector. For a nonzero invariant vector in an orbit component, the next step compares it with a vector of the generated span, modulo terms of smaller height. The two inclusions at a representative identify the orbit subspace with the span of the corresponding translates, so the passing to a quotient representative retains the same vector space. The orbit-height separation argument then proves membership. GlobalTheory.Hk3.HoweDuality.fixed_mem_span, RhoMemOfMkEq.lean:198–240.
  2. One component of the final pair is this lemma applied directly. For the other, take the Hermitian-adjoint swap of the two factors, including the transformed invariance and spanning hypotheses on $s$, then apply the same lemma. This explicit swap is what makes the two one-sided conclusions comparable. GlobalTheory.Hk3.howeUnram_holds, RhoMemOfMkEq.lean:250–256.

Moving to the square model

Here the assumptions are real Howe duality, the complete theta-local record, theta-integral agreement and local matching in the original tensor model. The desired target is local matching in the square model. These interfaces use different data. D044 supplies the companion assertion for agreement of integrals. GlobalTheory.T.twisted_local_factors_match, TwistedSquareLocalMatching.lean:88–89; GlobalTheory.SquareLM.twisted_square_local_matching, TwistedSquareLocalMatching.lean:18–29.

  1. For the chosen characters, spaces, frames and indices, take the common quotient and its two maps supplied by the real quotient theorem. Its invariant subspaces are trivial, and the scalar commutant assertion applies to its action. For each side form the subspace of finite Fock functions. Evaluation after the given map is bilinear in the finite function and the polynomial. Surjectivity on the algebraic Fock span identifies the resulting images as a spanning set in the target. GlobalTheory.SquareLM.twisted_square_local_matching, TwistedSquareLocalMatching.lean:20–56.
  2. The product-form theorem on these two subspaces returns a common factor, two functionals and their compatibility equations. Extend the functionals linearly to their ambient spaces. When evaluated on finite functions the extensions agree with the originals, so the spanning witnesses and matching identities required in the conclusion survive. It is the earlier common quotient and the prescribed test subspaces that supply the content behind this extension. GlobalTheory.SquareLM.twisted_square_local_matching, TwistedSquareLocalMatching.lean:57–81.
