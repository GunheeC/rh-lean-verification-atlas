T09 · Detecting a nonzero row on the theta evaluation grid

AI-written, AI cross-reviewed; no human sign-off; not a claim that RH is proved

Outlined · pending_review. The unit statement appears in manuscript v3, §5.19, p. 42. The input often denoted $F$ in this statement is the record ThetaLocalInputs. The other explicit inputs are the two spherical-span implications of unramified Howe duality and the convergent unitary Siegel–Weil statement. The latter occurs in this proof under the precise name SiegelWeilUnitaryConvergent. The doubled Eisenstein value has its own normalization and predicate.

What the grid must detect

Fix the exponent and $C_F\ge6510$. The size threshold is chosen before the finite-group family, the level character, the real datum, the compatible grid and model, and the row volume. Once these satisfy the size and Eisenstein conditions, fix a nonzero smooth vector $w$. A successful test consists of one real vector $\rho$ and one coefficient functional $\xi$, chosen at this stage. For every subsequent nonzero $t$ in the trivial-character evaluation space there must exist a column $\Psi$ and a grid vector $\Phi$ such that the row pairing converges and is nonzero. The pair $(\rho,\xi)$ is common to all these $t$; the witnesses $(\Psi,\Phi)$ may vary. The rank bound and the order of quantifiers are explicit in FunctionalsSeparate, and the two required conclusions are the conjuncts of Separates. See LevelPlusSubgroup.lean:65-71 and LevelPlusSubgroup.lean:83-91.

The pinned theorem first supplies the archimedean input realInputE_holds. This combines a large projection with an eigenvector map, using the lowest compact type, moment estimates, and the intertwining map recorded there. It then calls the separation theorem with this input and the three hypotheses above. A choice of a complex linear functional after choosing each $t$ would miss the strength of the target. See RealBranch.lean:231-235 and FunctionalsSeparateOfHoweUnramOfSiegelWeil.lean:17-19.

Producing the same tower for convergence and detection

The inner proof real_functionals_separate_of_hypothesis first obtains models of the separating data. A seesaw pair and extension by a twisted row produce the arrow from one model to the next. Passing to its multiplicity spaces gives a single row tower. Both the convergence and the rapid-decay arguments take this very tower as input. One cannot construct convergence for one action, choose an unrelated action with a nonzero period, and compose the two conclusions. See RealFunctionalsSeparateOfHypothesis.lean:15-24 and RealFunctionalsSeparateOfHypothesis.lean:28-30.

There is a concrete test for this compatibility at the top row. Specialize the tower to the full number of rows. Applying its vector identity to $t$ and $\Phi$ produces an automorphic function and a vector. The map restricting the row datum to the full index set is the identity. With that identification, the theta value at $t(w)$ equals the function evaluated at the tower vector. The tower gives convergence of the first expression. Cusp rapid decay for the resulting function permits the additional column integration against $\xi$. Thus the two analytic conditions apply to the row form used in the final test. This is separating_family_converges, ConvergenceOfSeparatingFamily.lean:14-26.

Nonvanishing and the final choice

For the nonvanishing part, periodNe_of_eigen uses the archimedean input with the hypotheses on Harish–Chandra modules, the Casselman–Wallach field and cusp rapid decay. A separate surjectivity argument brings a detecting class into the image of the core theta lift. It uses unramified Howe, the Weil representation and Siegel formula, convergent Siegel–Weil and the doubling inputs at the same local components. More particularly, the proof supplies surjectivity at even rank, the common grid and stage lattices, composites lifting finite combinations, and the column values at the definite top. This explains why both the local dictionary and the global integral enter the result. See twist_core_surjective_of_local_components, TwistCoreSurjectiveOfLocalComponents.lean:15-21, and RealFunctionalsSeparateOfHypothesis.lean:24-29.

Use the size-to-gap estimate, the separating models, rapid decay, the nonzero period, surjectivity and Bessel-period ascent to obtain weak row separation. The last step combines it with convergence and the principal-level good-row assertion. Its lower theorem also uses separation of points by theta lifts and the tensor description of the dual coefficient space over the real places. This is the passage from weak tests to the fixed $\rho,\xi$ of the statement; convergence persists for the column and datum realizing the nonzero value. See bessel_separate_of_step_gap, BesselSeparate.lean:8-11, and its application in RealFunctionalsSeparateOfHypothesis.lean:27-30.

The quantifier change has a short argument once the factorization factor_gap is available. At the maximum of the size-gap and weak-separation thresholds, it gives a factor $a(\rho,\xi)$ common to all rows. Each pairing factors as $a(\rho,\xi)l$, where $l$ may depend on the row, column and grid vector. If the evaluation space contains a nonzero $t_0$, weak separation gives a nonzero pairing on it, so the associated $a(\rho_0,\xi_0)$ is nonzero. Fix this pair. For another nonzero $t$, weak separation gives a column and datum with $l\ne0$. Using the fixed pair and those new column data gives $a(\rho_0,\xi_0)l\ne0$, with convergence from RowConv. If the evaluation space has no nonzero vector, its universal condition is empty and the zero functional suffices. This is separate_of_burGapPos, SepGap.lean:222-256.
