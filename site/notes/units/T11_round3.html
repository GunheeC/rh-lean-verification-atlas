T11 · One global comparison constant and local matching maps

AI-written, AI cross-reviewed; no human sign-off; not a claim that RH is proved

Outlined · pending_review. The unit statement is manuscript v3, §5.21, p. 43. Its formal implication has two antecedents, the doubled Eisenstein continuation record EisBL and the standard Siegel–Weil value. The latter supplies information at a distinguished point, whereas the first describes a continuation with its analytic estimates. Both occur in the calculation.

The quantifiers on matching

Fix the good number-field data and an admissible triple of Weil models. Locally one must find real maps $R$ and finite maps $R_f$ which match the models. Globally the assertion is stronger than an existence statement for one convenient match. The theta integrals must have the prescribed decay, and every choice of $R,R_f$ satisfying the matching conditions must have the constant and coinvariant properties. A second pair of clauses compares constants after adding a plane to one side or the other. The signs are chosen before the base data in each of those two clauses. See IsMatched, LocalMatching and ThetaIntegralsAgree, WeilTensorModels.lean:491-505 and WeilTensorModels.lean:581-592.

The pinned theorem first proves a version with a spherical lattice-model assumption ModSt. The final declaration fills it using the chart and its local model. This explains why no extra spherical-model hypothesis is passed by the caller of T11. The neighboring variant in the source accepts an additional input record which its actual proof does not use. See modSt2, ModSt2.lean:5-7, and theoremK_head2_of_mod and theoremK_head2, TheoremKOfMod.lean:14-27.

Construct the local comparison and control both integrals

At ramified finite places the proof takes coinvariant and multiplicity-one results and a finite ramified matching result. The split places supply finite split data. At inert places it uses the vacuum calculation from inert Howe duality together with a spherical-function result from ModSt and local representation existence. The spherical triple also uses geometric splitting. At the real places the construction takes the local lift, its differential calculation and its real comparison maps. All these inputs appear explicitly in the call to closeSph. Combining the ramified, split and inert cases gives the finite record KFin; together with the real maps this is precisely the information used to produce a match. See TheoremKOfMod.lean:15-20, CloseSph.lean:28-43, CloseSph.lean:129-135 and matching_of_parts as applied in AgreeOfLeaves.lean:7-8.

For the global calculation the Siegel set supplies height and decay estimates. On the unprimed side the target asks for convergence on every Schwartz vector. On the primed test space the explicit estimate bounds the kernel times an arbitrary power of the height in the first variable by a polynomial in the height of the other variable. The degree and constant may depend on the first power. The integrated primed function is moderate. In the doubling calculation the standard value feeds the anisotropic comparison, and EisBL supplies continuation. These are joined with the elementary-section data before the normalized zeta integral, the local factors and the nonzero bottom contribution are used. This ordering keeps analytic continuation separate from the proof that there is a nonzero test. See WeilTensorModels.lean:498-505 and CloseSph.lean:103-117, CloseSph.lean:118-135.

The scalar, the quotient and the two tower steps

For a particular match a scalar $c$ must satisfy $I(\Phi)(h)=cI'(\Phi')(h)$ at every $h$ and for all matching pairs of tests. OneConst records a nonzero primed test integral, existence and uniqueness of a scalar satisfying this identity, and nonvanishing of every such scalar. Thus a comparison established only after choosing one test with a convenient value is too weak. At a good place the auxiliary quotient record identifies the kernel on test vectors with augmentation on both sides. It asserts surjectivity from the primed local test space. A functional annihilating augmentation consequently descends through the finite quotient on test vectors. The global primed test space is stable under the primed action. These are the precise clauses of IsConst, OneConst and Coinv at WeilTensorModels.lean:491-512. The proof supplies finite coinvariants and joins these assertions in agree_of_leaves, CloseSph.lean:145-150 and AgreeOfLeaves.lean:7-8.

Keep the two sign triples distinct when comparing adjacent ranks. Write $F$ for the signed product stepFac formed from the two plane ideals and the local factors, and $L$ for the specified character factor. In the beta step put $h=\lfloor(a-b-2)_+/2\rfloor$. Then $uCF(-h)=1$ and $cL(h+1)=u c_1$. In the alpha step put $h=\lfloor(b+2-a')_+/2\rfloor$. The relation becomes $c=CF(1-h)L(h)c_1$. The rank subtractions inside $h$ are in the natural numbers. The nonzero $C$ can depend on the plane geometry and real maps and is fixed before the later characters and matching constants. The two endpoint constructions contribute these laws; the final pair uses the comparison record and the local matching record as separate conjuncts. See WeilTensorModels.lean:552-567, WeilTensorModels.lean:571-592, CloseSph.lean:183-193 and AgreeOfLeaves.lean:7-8.
