The rank-three ordinary deformation datum has a specific saturated ideal in its universal coefficient ring. One assertion identifies a crystalline consequence of killing the ideal. A second says that a prescribed class of crystalline infinitesimal tests kills it. GlobalTheory.T.saturatedOrdinaryIdeal_cuts_out_crystalline_locus (SaturatedOrdinaryIdealCutsOutCrystallineLocusStatement.lean:6–7).

Fix the ideal before choosing a point

Choose a prime $\ell$ and a characteristic-zero complete discrete valuation ring $O$ finite over the $\ell$-adic integers with the specified algebra structure. Fix the rank-three datum $D$ and its universal object $U$. The ordinary ideal is saturated at the element called del: an element belongs to $I_Y$ precisely if multiplication by some nonnegative power of del puts it in the ordinary ideal. The exponent is existential for each element. Both conclusions concern this very $I_Y$ in $U.R$ under the local published-theorem hypothesis. GlobalTheory.T.saturatedOrdinaryIdeal_cuts_out_crystalline_locus (SaturatedOrdinaryIdealCutsOutCrystallineLocusStatement.lean:6–7). Go from the ideal to a crystalline point

Take a compatible finite algebra $B$ over the $\ell$-adic rationals and an $O$-algebra map from the universal ring annihilating the ideal. The desired conclusion concerns the pushed representation. The source extracts its linear-group component on the specified subgroup, checks the auxiliary conditions, and tests it after further scalar extension. For an extension $B'$ the composite map still kills the ideal since the original map did. The specialized crystalline point theorem supplies a representation over $B'$; an entrywise comparison identifies it with the scalar extension of the component already selected. This identification is essential for applying the local crystalline criterion. UpperTheory.V.Loc.Dat3.PtsCr (IsInertiaInfiniteCyclotomic.lean:140–143). GlobalTheory.OrdLoc.ptsCr_IY (SaturatedOrdIdealPointsIsLCrystalline.lean:17–50).

Go from an admissible test to the ideal

In this direction the target data include a finite extension $E$, an algebra $B$ finite over the $\ell$-adic rationals, and a projection to $E$ whose kernel elements are nilpotent. Assume the designated deformation condition on the pushed representation relative to that projection. The proof constructs the compatible fraction-field tower for $O,E,B$, using injectivity of the coefficient maps, and applies the reduction for this particular test to conclude $I_Y\subseteq\ker f$. The two implications have different input predicates and target algebras, so treating the second as a reversal of the first would miss a substantive comparison. The wrapper simply supplies this pair; a related local comparison involving finite cocycles is D082. UpperTheory.V.Loc.Dat3.CrPts (IsInertiaInfiniteCyclotomic.lean:145–151).
