Quantified hypotheses

V. Fix any primitive Dirichlet character $\psi$, any real $0<\varepsilon<1/52$, and any $e:\mathbb R\to\mathbb N$ satisfying $u^{2+8\varepsilon}\le 2^{e(u)}\le 2u^{2+8\varepsilon}$ for every $u\ge1$. There exist real $J\ge1$ and $X_0$ such that for every $X\ge X_0$ and every window pair $(q,d)$ at $y=X^{1/J}$ there exists $Y\ge y$ with $Y^2\le X$ and the following property. For every $Y\le x\le Y^2$ and every number field $E$ of type $(\psi,e(x),q,d)$, put $t=\lfloor x^{401}\rfloor_+$ and $b=\exp(x^\varepsilon/2)$. For every algebraic closure $\Omega/E$ and every allowed system of local data at the rational primes, all the Selmer lengths $L_{\ell,a}$ are finite for $1\le a<t$, and for every finite set $S$ of primes,

$$\sum_{\ell\in S}\log(\ell)\sum_{1\le a<t} L_{\ell,a}\le b .$$

Here $L_{\ell,a}$ denotes the finite length represented by shaLen for the indicated local datum and twist $a$. This quantification includes $\ell=2$. A window pair consists of primes $q\equiv3\pmod8$ and $d\equiv7\pmod{16}$. With $h=y^{\varepsilon/5}$ it satisfies $h/2<\log q\le h$, $h\log^2(y)/2<\log d\le h\log^2(y)$, $q\mid d+1$, and $p\mid d+1$ for every odd prime $p\le h$. The field type requires an embedding into a cyclotomic field of order $N$ divisible by $2^{e(x)+2}$, by the modulus of $\psi$, and by $q,d$, with the following fixing group. For an automorphism acting on a chosen primitive root by exponent $a$, it fixes the embedded field pointwise precisely if $a\equiv\pm1\pmod{2^{e(x)+2}}$, $\psi(a)=1$, and both Jacobi symbols at $q,d$ equal $+1$. Conclusions and proof outline

For any $s\in\mathbb C$, a zero of $\zeta(s)$ other than $1$ and the negative even integers has $\Re s=1/2$ in both public formulations. For every positive modulus and every character $\chi$ of that modulus, $\Re s>1/2$ and $(\chi\ne\chi_0\ \text{or}\ s\ne1)$ imply $L(s,\chi)\ne0$. Every zero of such a character in $0<\Re s<1$ has $\Re s=1/2$, also upon restriction to primitive characters. The five source declarations are in Main.lean:4–12. P001 · P002 · P003 · P004 · P005

1. Put both estimates on a single host. Fix a primitive $\psi$ and let $\Lambda=\operatorname{cyc}(\psi)$. For any admissible $\varepsilon$, choose the dyadic exponent function $e$ in V and receive its $J,X_0$. Fix the field-frame constant $c\ge1$, set $a=2+8\varepsilon$ and $k=c^{1/a}$. For the requested lower endpoint $y$ and B1 threshold $Y_1$, choose $M\ge\max(1,y,Y_1,k^3,k^{400})$, then $X\ge\max(X_0,M^J)$. B1 selects $q,d$ at $X^{1/J}$. V returns $W\ge X^{1/J}\ge M$. Put $Z=kW$. This is the order in winPairs, CharacterBoxes/WinPairs.lean:23–37. At the B2 application the uniform scale threshold also forces $4\psi.N\le\lfloor Y^{\varepsilon/5}\rfloor$, $q\ne3$ and $e\ge e_0$ (supplyOfAdmOf, ShortNorms/supplyOfAdmOf.lean:39–93). For $Z\le x\le Z^{3/2}$ put $u=x/k$. Then $W\le u\le W^2$, $x^a\le c2^{e(u)}\le2x^a$, $x^{400}\le u^{401}$ and $\exp(u^\varepsilon/2)\le\exp(x^\varepsilon/2)$. The good prime pair persists on the restricted interval. B2–B8 give the field, ranks on that exact frame, admissibility and the good host with its selected character classes. The eventual rank theorem applied to this frame and its RankVal certificate (supplyOfAdmOf.lean:38,129–135) shows that its rank $t$ is at most $\lfloor x^{400}\rfloor_+$, so nonnegativity of each summand restricts V to $t$ on that same field. The request for this cyclic level has its two required components at each member scale, the explicit-formula bounds and the finite Selmer-sum bound. This gives $\mathrm{HostExW}(3/2,G_\psi)$ on arbitrarily far windows. See CharacterBoxes/WinPairs.lean:5–81 and CharacterBoxes/SupplyChi.lean:8–16. B07 · D511 · D513

2. Put division and slope on the same family. Choose one witness of F. Its final field gives the closed $N$ estimate. Here the kernel-bound assembly is outlined. C5, C12, C6 and C4 join these with the closed nonnegative size bound. The toRows_alt conversion gives the kernel bound needed for C3. Green and the remaining closed implications use the same selection. See shape_closed_link_n_of_green, ThirdPlaceSeries/TopShape.lean:33–55, and B05. Intersect the nine closed conditions by taking the minimum of their positive $\varepsilon$ thresholds, then, at that $\varepsilon$, the componentwise supremum of their required largeness parameters. Choose a design above this common parameter on the intersection of the arithmetic class with the request class. The resulting family and the constants of the chosen design satisfy all nine conditions simultaneously. The rank equality and the finite Selmer sums from the request give the local $\mathrm{DIVinN}$ estimates via $\mathrm{LinkN}$. 3. Use the tied family on its windows. Fix a sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$. The request supplies $\mathrm{EFamC}(\Lambda,F_m)$ and arbitrarily far intervals $[Z,Z^{3/2}]$ in the family window. By P017 with list A and the pointwise P021 lattice bound used in B10, there is one eventual threshold, after which the whole interval satisfies

$$\mathcal G_\Lambda(x)\le x^{1/2+(e_D+e_L+7)\varepsilon}=x^{1/2+9\varepsilon}.$$

At one scale the balance is a finite character identity. Averaging it over the subgroup retains the selected characters, bounds the complement, and leaves the four errors in the B09 estimate. The rank, cardinality and width parameters bound those errors at a half-power with the stated exponent loss. The family version chooses its constants before the scale. The separate notes give the one-scale, numerical, and threshold arguments. Finite average · Lattice error · One scale · Family estimate

4. Exclude a zero and descend to the public assertions. Suppose $L(\rho,\psi)=0$ with $\Re\rho>1/2$ away from the principal pole. Choose $1/2<\theta<\min(1,\Re\rho)$, and then an admissible $\varepsilon$ with $9\varepsilon<\theta-1/2$. For windows far enough to exceed the threshold and 1 the sum is at most $x^\theta$. In the open strip a zero of a character in the level contributes its multiplicity to the Mellin residue with a positive level weight. The contributions of coincident zeros have the same scalar factor, hence their sum is nonzero, and at a real zero the residue is positive. This Mellin argument is a sketch. The implication used here is the level criterion formB (P018, PrimitiveCosets/Imports.lean:8–19), which excludes a level zero to the right of $\theta$ in the strip. The level criterion also uses classical nonvanishing for $\Re s\ge1$ away from the exception. As $\psi$ occurs in its cyclic level, either range contradicts the assumed zero. See v4 Proposition 6.32, p. 83. P018 · P022 · P023

Inducing a primitive character adds finitely many Euler factors $1-\psi(p)p^{-s}$. Each is nonzero when $\Re s>0$, so change of level gives P003 with its stated principal exception. An open-strip zero left of the line descends to the primitive character and reflects across $s\mapsto1-s$ to a zero of the inverse primitive character right of the line. The gamma factors are finite and nonzero in this strip. The right half is already excluded, proving P004 and its restriction P005. At modulus one the strip assertion applies to $\zeta$. A zero at $\Re s\le0$ away from $0$ and the negative even integers would, by the completed zeta functional equation, give a zero at $1-s$, where $\Re(1-s)\ge1$. Also $\zeta(0)=-1/2$. This gives P002 and its equivalent Mathlib formulation P001. See CharacterBoxes/Imprimitive.lean:5–35 and CharacterBoxes/ClaimStrip.lean:59–102. P003 · P004 · P001

Comparison with the formal implication. The pinned UpperTheory.Top.zero_free_all_of_green_v_win and UpperTheory.Top.every_character_of_green_v_win explicitly receive Green, V and the nonempty record F. They invoke the internal host and shape constructions. The manuscript exposes A–C as separate assumptions while tracing that argument and closes with the five public statements. Its written proof does not construct the global witnesses V or F. A referee can first follow the common selection from the closed $N$ hypothesis through the rank equality and $N'$-slope step, checking that each supplies the input of the next implication on the very family used in the window argument.
