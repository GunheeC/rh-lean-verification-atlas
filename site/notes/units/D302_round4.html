D302 · How one divided power generates a crystalline graded piece

In the specified period algebra, the degree-$r$ filtration quotient is generated over the coefficient ring by the $r$th divided power, with coefficient ambiguity exactly the ideal generated by $\xi$. A separate assertion clears a power of the coefficient prime for each filtered element. GlobalTheory.T.crystalline_graded_pieces_description (CrystallineGradedPiecesDescriptionStatement.lean:8–9).

Identify the three clauses

Fix a prime $\ell$, the specified period algebra and a nonnegative degree $r$. For $z$ in filtration $r$, first find $w$ in the coefficient ring $W$ so that $z-w\,d_r$ belongs to filtration $r+1$; here $d_r$ denotes the indicated divided power and coefficients are mapped to the crystalline ring. Second, a multiple $w\,d_r$ belongs to that next filtration precisely when $w$ is divisible by $\xi$. Third, some $\ell^N z$ is the image of $\xi^r w'$ for a possibly different coefficient. The last exponent can depend on $z$. The first two statements concern the integral quotient and do not require choosing a uniform clearing exponent. Separate the degree-$r$ summand

The source writes a filtered element in the span of divided powers of index at least $r$. On a generator with strictly greater index, use coefficient zero. At index $r$ use coefficient one. Addition and scalar multiplication carry these choices through span induction, since the higher filtration is closed under both operations. The power-clearing argument is separate. On a divided-power generator its factorial relates the generator to a power of $\xi$; isolate the prime-power factor of the factorial. The induction over finite sums allows a common exponent for the terms of the element currently under consideration. Calculate the coefficient kernel

For $w\,d_r$ in the next filtration apply power clearing there, multiply by $r!$, then use the identity for $r!d_r$. Injectivity of the coefficient map lets one work in $W$ and cancel $\xi^r$. The resulting relation $\ell^N w=\xi(r!v)$ requires an additional divisibility argument modulo $\ell^N$ to write $r!v=\ell^N y$. Cancelling $\ell^N$ now yields $w=\xi y$. Conversely multiplying $d_r$ by $\xi$ gives $(r+1)d_{r+1}$, in filtration $r+1$. This last implication uses only an integral identity. The period divisibility problem in D245 has a different target ring and a Frobenius hypothesis.
