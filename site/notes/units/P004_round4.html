The public assertion applies to every character of a positive modulus. Its proof selects the open-strip component of a conjunction. The analytic reduction behind that component must handle characters induced from smaller conductors. LFunction_eq_zero_re (Main.lean:10–10).

Select the middle component

In P006 the three components are grouped as $H\land(S\land Z)$. Taking the second projection and then the first gives $S$. Write down the domain of that component before applying it. The modulus is nonzero, the character is arbitrary, and the point is strictly between the vertical lines at zero and one. P007 has an extra primitivity hypothesis. Projection from that assertion would give a different public statement. LFunction_eq_zero_re (Main.lean:10–10). DirichletLZerosOnLine (Statements.lean:10–12). claimAll (MainTheorems.lean:15–16).

Reduce the vanishing to a primitive character

For a character induced from conductor $M$, change of level expresses the old L-function as a primitive L-function times a finite product. If a prime factor in that product vanished, $\psi(p)p^{-s}$ would equal one. Its norm is bounded by $p^{-\Re s}<1$ since the character value has norm at most one. Thus the product cannot absorb the zero. The change-of-level identity also needs $s\ne1$, supplied by the upper strip inequality. Claim.prim_zero (ClaimStrip.lean:23–33). Claim.factor_ne_zero (ClaimStrip.lean:7–21).

Follow the reflected character

When $\Re s<1/2$, nonvanishing of the archimedean gamma factor converts the primitive zero into a completed zero. Apply the functional equation for the inverse primitive character at $1-s$ and return to the ordinary L-function there. Now the half-plane statement applies at the conductor and to that inverse character. The exception is discharged by $1-s\ne1$, which follows from $s\ne0$. For $\Re s>1/2$ the half-plane statement applies directly at the original modulus using $s\ne1$. Equality is the remaining case. The proof here uses P009 as its arithmetic input. Claim.prim_reflect (ClaimStrip.lean:42–57). Claim.strip (ClaimStrip.lean:59–73).
